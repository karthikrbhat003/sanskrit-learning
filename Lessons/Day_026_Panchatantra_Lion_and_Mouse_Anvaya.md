## 🌟 Subhāṣitam (Good Saying of the Day)

**संस्कृतम्:**
> न कश्चित् कस्यचिन्मित्रं न कश्चित् कस्यचिद्रिपुः ।
> व्यवहारेण जायन्ते मित्राणि रिपवस्तथा ॥

**Transliteration (IAST):**
> *na kaścit kasyacin mitraṃ na kaścit kasyacid ripuḥ |*
> *vyavahāreṇa jāyante mitrāṇi ripavas tathā ||*

**English Translation:**
> No one is inherently anyone's friend, nor is anyone inherently an enemy. It is solely through one's conduct and actions that friends and enemies are made.

## 📖 मूल-कथा (Original Panchatantra Prose)

### **सिंह-मूषक-कथा (The Lion and the Mouse)**

**एकस्मिन् वने एकः पराक्रमी सिंहः वृक्षाधः सुखेन सुप्तः आसीत्।**
> *Ekasmin vane ekaḥ parākramī siṃhaḥ vṛkṣādhaḥ sukhena suptaḥ āsīt.*
> In a forest, a mighty lion was sleeping peacefully under a tree.

**तस्मिन्नेव काले एकः लघुः मूषकः तत्र आगच्छत्, सिंहस्य शरीरे च अक्क्रीडत्।**
> *Tasminn eva kāle ekaḥ laghuḥ mūṣakaḥ tatra āgacchat, siṃhasya śarīre ca akrīḍat.*
> At that very time, a small mouse came there and played on the lion's body.

**तेन सिंहस्य निद्रा भग्ना अभवत्। सः क्रुद्धः सन् मूषकम् स्व-नखैः अगृह्णात्।**
> *Tena siṃhasya nidrā bhagnā abhavat. Saḥ kruddhaḥ san mūṣakam sva-nakhaiḥ agṛhṇāt.*
> By that, the lion's sleep was broken. Enraged, he caught the mouse in his claws.

**मूषकः भय-विह्वलः सन् अवदत्—"भोः मृगराज! माम् क्षमस्व! कदाचित् अहम् अपि भवतः साहाय्यम् करिष्यामि।"**
> *Mūṣakaḥ bhaya-vihvalaḥ san avadat—"Bhoḥ mṛgarāja! Mām kṣamasva! Kadācit aham api bhavataḥ sāhāyyam kariṣyāmi."*
> Trembling with fear, the mouse pleaded—"O King of Beasts! Forgive me! Sometime in the future, even I might be of assistance to you."

**सिंहः अहसत्, दयया च तम् अमुञ्चत्।**
> *Siṃhaḥ ahasat, dayayā ca tam amuñcat.*
> The lion laughed and released him out of compassion.

**कतिपय-दिनानन्तरम् सः सिंहः व्याधस्य जाले बद्धः अभवत्। सः उच्चैः अगर्जत्।**
> *Katipaya-dinānantaram saḥ siṃhaḥ vyādhasya jāle baddhaḥ abhavat. Saḥ uccaiḥ agarjat.*
> A few days later, that lion became trapped in a hunter's net. He roared loudly.

**तस्य गर्जनम् श्रुत्वा मूषकः शीघ्रम् तत्र आजगाम (आगच्छत्)।**
> *Tasya garjanam śrutvā mūṣakaḥ śīghram tatra ājagāma (āgacchat).*
> Hearing his roar, the mouse quickly arrived there.

**मूषकः स्व-तीक्ष्ण-दन्तैः जालम् अकृन्तत्, सिंहम् च अबन्धनात् अमोचयत्।**
> *Mūṣakaḥ sva-tīkṣṇa-dantaiḥ jālam akṛntat, siṃham ca abandhanāt amocayat.*
> The mouse cut the net with his sharp teeth and freed the lion from captivity.

**सिंहः मूषकस्य कृतज्ञः अभवत्। तौ परस्परम् मित्रत्वम् प्राप्नुताम्।**
> *Siṃhaḥ mūṣakasya kṛtajñaḥ abhavat. Tau parasparam mitratvam prāpnutām.*
> The lion became deeply grateful to the mouse. The two attained lifelong friendship with one another.

## 🔍 अन्वयः & पदच्छेदः (Sentence Dissection & Prose Order)

In Sanskrit poetry and classical prose, words are often arranged for rhythm and emphasis. **अन्वयः (Anvaya)** is the logical reordering of words into standard grammatical sequence:
$$\textbf{Subject (कर्ता)} \longrightarrow \textbf{Indirect Objects / Circumstances} \longrightarrow \textbf{Direct Object (कर्म)} \longrightarrow \textbf{Verb (क्रिया)}$$

### Example Analysis:
*Original Text*: **मूषकः स्व-तीक्ष्ण-दन्तैः जालम् अकृन्तत्।**
* **पदच्छेदः (Word Segmentation)**: मूषकः (1st Sg.) $+$ स्व-तीक्ष्ण-दन्तैः (3rd Pl.) $+$ जालम् (2nd Sg.) $+$ अकृन्तत् (Past 3rd Sg.)
* **अन्वयः (Prose Syntax)**:
  1. *Who performed action?* $\rightarrow$ **मूषकः** (Subject)
  2. *With what instrument?* $\rightarrow$ **स्व-तीक्ष्ण-दन्तैः** (Instrument - with sharp teeth)
  3. *What was acted upon?* $\rightarrow$ **जालम्** (Object - net)
  4. *What was the action?* $\rightarrow$ **अकृन्तत्** (Verb - cut)

## 💡 व्याकरण-विशेषः (The Ktvā Gerund - क्त्वा प्रत्ययः)

Notice the word **श्रुत्वा (śrutvā = having heard)** in the story.
In Sanskrit, when the same subject performs two consecutive actions, the first action takes the **क्त्वा (ktvā)** suffix ("*having done X, he did Y*"):
* **श्रु (hear) $+$ क्त्वा $=$ श्रुत्वा** (*having heard*)
* **गम् (go) $+$ क्त्वा $=$ गत्वा** (*having gone*)
* **पठ् (read) $+$ क्त्वा $=$ पठित्वा** (*having read*)
* **दृश् (see) $+$ क्त्वा $=$ दृष्ट्वा** (*having seen*)
* **कृ (do) $+$ क्त्वा $=$ कृत्वा** (*having done*)

*Example*: *मूषकः गर्जनम् **श्रुत्वा** तत्र **अगच्छत्**।* (The mouse, **having heard** the roar, **went** there).

### Exercise 1: Answer in Sanskrit based on the story
1. सिंहः कुत्र सुप्तः आसीत्?

3. सिंहः कस्य जाले बद्धः अभवत्?
4. मूषकः केन जालम् अकृन्तत्?

2. पी (पा - to drink) $+$ क्त्वा $\rightarrow$ _________ (Having drunk)
3. लिख् (to write) $+$ क्त्वा $\rightarrow$ _________ (Having written)

## 🔑 Answers to Exercises

### Answers to Exercise 1:
1. सिंहः **वृक्षाधः (वृक्षस्य अधः)** सुखेन सुप्तः आसीत्।
2. सिंहस्य शरीरे **मूषकः** अक्रीडत्।
3. सिंहः **व्याधस्य जाले** बद्धः अभवत्।
4. मूषकः **स्व-तीक्ष्ण-दन्तैः** जालम् अकृन्तत्।

### Answers to Exercise 2:
1. खाद् $+$ क्त्वा $=$ **खादित्वा** (*khāditvā*)
2. पा $+$ क्त्वा $=$ **पीत्वा** (*pītvā*)
3. लिख् $+$ क्त्वा $=$ **लिखित्वा** (*likhitvā*)
4. **पुस्तकम् पठित्वा बालकः गृहम् अगच्छत्।** (*Pustakam paṭhitvā bālakaḥ gṛham agacchat.*)
