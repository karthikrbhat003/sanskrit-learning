# Day 033: रामायण-प्रवेशः (Valmiki Ramayana & The Anuṣṭubh Meter)

Welcome to Day 33! Today, we step into the grand world of the **आदिकाव्यम् (Ādikāvyam - The First Epic Poem of Humanity)**: the **रामायणम् (Rāmāyaṇa)** composed by Sage Valmiki.

We will analyze the birth of Sanskrit poetry, study authentic verses from the *Saṅkṣepa Rāmāyaṇam*, and learn the universal meter of Sanskrit epics: **अनुष्टुप् छन्दः (The Anuṣṭubh Meter)**.

---

## 🌟 The First Shloka in Human History (आदिश्लोकः)

When Sage Valmiki witnessed a hunter mercilessly strike down one of a pair of devoted Krauncha birds, his profound compassion (*śoka*) spontaneously burst forth as the world's very first poetic meter (*śloka*):

**संस्कृतम्:**
> मा निषाद प्रतिष्ठां त्वमगमः शाश्वतीः समाः ।
> यत्क्रौञ्चमिथुनादेकमवधीः काममोहितम् ॥

**Transliteration (IAST):**
> *mā niṣāda pratiṣṭhāṃ tvam agamaḥ śāśvatīḥ samāḥ |*
> *yat krauñcamithunād ekam avadhīḥ kāmamohitam ||*

**English Translation:**
> "May you never find peace or resting place for eternal years, O hunter! For you have slain one of this loving pair of birds intoxicated with love!"

## 📖 मूल-श्लोकाः (Original Valmiki Ramayana - Bala Kanda 1.1)

### **संक्षेप-रामायणम् (The Ideal Human Being)**

Sage Valmiki asks Sage Narada about the greatest ideals of human character:

#### **Verse 1: The Inquiry**
**संस्कृतम्:**
> को न्वस्मिन् साम्प्रतं लोके गुणवान् कश्च वीर्यवान् ।
> धर्मज्ञश्च कृतज्ञश्च सत्यवाक्यो दृढव्रतः ॥

**Transliteration (IAST):**
> *ko nv asmin sāmprataṃ loke guṇavān kaś ca vīryavān |*
> *dharmajñaś ca kṛtajñaś ca satyavākyo dṛḍhavrataḥ ||*

**पदच्छेदः (Word Separation):**
> कः $+$ नु $+$ अस्मिन् $+$ साम्प्रतम् $+$ लोके $+$ गुणवान् $+$ कः $+$ च $+$ वीर्यवान् ।
> धर्मज्ञः $+$ च $+$ कृतज्ञः $+$ च $+$ सत्यवाक्यः $+$ दृढव्रतः ॥

**अन्वयः & English Translation:**
> *अस्मिन् लोके साम्प्रतं कः गुणवान्, कः च वीर्यवान्, धर्मज्ञः च, कृतज्ञः च, सत्यवाक्यः, दृढव्रतः (अस्ति)?*
> "In this world today, who is endowed with all virtues? Who is valorous, knower of righteousness, grateful for even the smallest kindness, truthful in speech, and firm in noble vows?"

---

#### **Verse 2: Narada's Revelation**
**संस्कृतम्:**
> इक्ष्वाकुवंशप्रभवो रामो नाम जनैः श्रुतः ।
> नियतात्मा महावीर्यो द्युतिमान् धृतिमान् वशी ॥

**Transliteration (IAST):**
> *ikṣvāku-vaṃśa-prabhavo rāmo nāma janaiḥ śrutaḥ |*
> *niyatātmā mahāvīryo dyutimān dhṛtimān vaśī ||*

**पदच्छेदः (Word Separation):**
> इक्ष्वाकु-वंश-प्रभवः $+$ रामः $+$ नाम $+$ जनैः $+$ श्रुतः ।
> नियतात्मा $+$ महावीर्यः $+$ द्युतिमान् $+$ धृतिमान् $+$ वशी ॥

**अन्वयः & English Translation:**
> *इक्ष्वाकु-वंश-प्रभवः, जनैः 'रामः' नाम श्रुतः, नियतात्मा, महावीर्यः, द्युतिमान्, धृतिमान्, वशी (अस्ति)।*
> "Born in the illustrious Ikshvaku dynasty, renowned among people by the sacred name of **Rāma**, He is self-controlled, of immense valor, radiant with majesty, steadfast in courage, and master of his senses!"

## 💡 छन्दः-शास्त्रम् (The Architecture of the Anuṣṭubh Meter)

Over 90% of the Ramayana, Mahabharata, Puranas, and Bhagavad Gita are composed in the **अनुष्टुप् (Anuṣṭubh)** meter.

### Structure of an Anuṣṭubh Shloka:
* **Total Syllables**: 32 syllables divided into **4 Quarters (Pādas)** of **8 syllables each** ($4 \times 8 = 32$).
* **The Universal Metric Rhythm Formula**:
  $$\text{In every 8-syllable quarter, the \textbf{5th syllable is always Short (Laghu $\cup$)} and the \textbf{6th syllable is Long (Guru $-$)}}.$$

$$\begin{array}{|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|c|}
\hline
\text{1} & \text{2} & \text{3} & \text{4} & \textbf{5} & \textbf{6} & \textbf{7} & \text{8} \\
\hline
\text{any} & \text{any} & \text{any} & \text{any} & \mathbf{\cup \text{ (Short)}} & \mathbf{- \text{ (Long)}} & \mathbf{\cup / -} & \text{pause} \\
\hline
\end{array}$$

3. **धर्मज्ञः $+$ च**
